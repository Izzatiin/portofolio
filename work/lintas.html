Lintas

Helping first-time riders plan a trip on Jakarta's commuter rail without asking a stranger for help.

Overview

Lintas is a capstone concept for a trip planner built around one moment: a person's first ride on the commuter line. Our team spent a semester riding along with new commuters to see where they got lost and why.

I led the interaction design and built the prototype we tested every week, updating it before each round of sessions.

The problem

The commuter rail moves huge crowds every day, but it assumes you already know the system. Route maps show lines, not transfers, and signs differ from station to station. New riders, often students or workers who just moved to the city, end up asking strangers or following the crowd.

“First-time riders were not confused by the route. They were anxious about the transfer, the platform, and the moment the doors close.”

Key insight from our ride-along sessions

Research

We joined new riders on their trips, interviewed station staff, and read through reviews of existing transit apps to find recurring frustrations.

What we learned

  • Riders checked their phones most often right before a transfer, not at the start of the trip.
  • Platform numbers mattered more than line colors.
  • People wanted reassurance that they were still on the right train.

Design

We focused on the transfer moment and turned the trip into a sequence of steps that follows the rider from station to station.

From maps to steps

Early tests compared a map-first layout with a step-first layout. Riders understood the step view faster and looked at their phone less while walking between platforms.

Designing for the platform

Each step shows the platform number in large type, the direction of the train, and how many stops remain. A gentle vibration reminds the rider one stop before getting off.

Outcome

By the final round of testing, riders could complete a trip with two transfers without asking anyone for help.

Calmer transfers

Testers pointed to the platform-first step view as the part that made them feel confident.

Presented to mentors

We shared the prototype and research with our faculty advisor and industry mentors at the capstone showcase.

Reflection

With more time, I would test Lintas during rush hour, when crowds change how people hold and use their phones.

I also learned to share rough prototypes earlier. Our weekly tests caught problems that weeks of discussion did not.
