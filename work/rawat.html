Overview

Rawat started with a family problem. My grandmother takes several medicines a day, and we kept calling each other to ask whether she had taken them.

Rawat lets an older adult confirm a dose with one large button, and lets the family see it without a phone call.

The problem

Most reminder apps assume the patient manages everything alone. In many families, care is shared, and the older adult may not be comfortable with small text or crowded menus.

“Parents did not want to feel watched. They wanted their children to stop worrying.”

Key insight from family interviews

Research

I interviewed families and observed how parents took their medicine in the morning and evening, and how their children checked in.

What I learned

  • Parents preferred one big, familiar action over notifications with several options.
  • Children wanted a quiet signal, not loud alarms.
  • Pill names were hard to read, but pill colors and shapes were easy to remember.

Design

I designed two connected experiences: a very simple one for the parent and a calm overview for the family.

One button for the parent

The parent's screen shows a photo of the medicine and one large “Taken” button. Text starts large and follows the phone's accessibility settings.

A calm view for the family

Family members see a simple daily timeline. They only get an alert when a dose is missed for more than an hour.

Outcome

In testing, parents confirmed their doses without help after a short introduction, and their children checked the app instead of calling.

Less checking in

Families said the timeline replaced most of their “have you taken your medicine?” calls.

Accessibility habits

The patterns from this project now shape how I handle text size and touch targets in every project.

Reflection

Designing for my own family made me careful about privacy and dignity. Next, I would explore voice confirmation for parents with low vision.
